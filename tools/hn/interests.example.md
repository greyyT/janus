# HN digest interests (example)

Copy this file to `tools/hn/interests.md` and make it yours; `tools/hn/hn.sh` uses `interests.md` when it exists and this example otherwise. The triage agent sorts every front-page story into one tier using it, and the writers read the listener section to pitch each story. When a story fits two tiers, pick the higher one. Each run's `report.md` lists what was skipped and why, so misses show up there.

## Listener

Name:

The `Name:` line is optional; when filled, the episode greets the listener by name.

A software engineer with a few years of experience who uses AI coding tools daily and wants real depth behind them. Not a beginner: skip the basics and keep the substance.

## Deep: full two-person discussion

- Web and application engineering: languages, frameworks, browsers, performance, accessibility.
- AI engineering: LLMs, coding agents, AI workflows and tooling, evals, AI products and their economics.
- Engineering craft: software architecture, system design, databases, distributed systems, reliability, testing, debugging.
- Developer productivity, team process, engineering management, product judgment.
- Tech careers: hiring, interviews, compensation, remote work.
- Learning, focus, reading, writing, habits, attention. These are must-read candidates.
- Security incidents and lessons that affect working engineers.

## Light: the event plus a short exchange, no deep dive

- World news, geopolitics, economics, tech regulation.
- Big-tech business moves: earnings, acquisitions, leadership changes, antitrust.
- Notable science or space news.

## Brief: one-line mention in "Quick hits"

- Interesting but off-focus software stories: nostalgia, retro computing, niche language releases, Show HN projects outside the deep topics.

## Skip

- Hardware hobby and electronics projects.
- Deep math, physics, biology or history with no link to the listener's work or life.
- Consumer gear reviews.
- Anything else unrelated to the listener's work, ways of working, or the major news of the day.
